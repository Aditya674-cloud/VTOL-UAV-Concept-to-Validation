# 0. Requirements and Mission

## 0.1 Purpose of This Chapter

This chapter defines the purpose, mission, initial requirements, system boundaries, assumptions, and overall development philosophy of the VTOL UAV project.

The purpose of defining these requirements before detailed design is to prevent the aircraft geometry, propulsion system, electronics, control system, and software architecture from being developed independently.

The aircraft is treated as a complete engineering system.

The design process therefore follows:

**Mission → Requirements → Configuration → Preliminary Sizing → Aerodynamics → Propulsion → Mechanical Design → Structural Analysis → Flight Dynamics → Control → Electronics → Perception → Collision Avoidance → Simulation → Validation**

The numerical values presented in this chapter are classified as either:

* **Requirement** — a target that the final system is expected to satisfy.
* **Assumption** — an initial value used to begin engineering calculations.
* **Calculated** — a value obtained from an engineering model or equation.
* **Selected** — a value chosen after comparing available options.
* **Validated** — a value supported by simulation, experiment, or another appropriate verification method.
* **TBD** — not yet determined.

This distinction is important because the first aircraft design is not expected to be final. Aircraft design is an iterative process in which mass, geometry, propulsion, aerodynamics, structure, energy storage, control and mission performance influence one another.

# 0.3 Why a Hybrid VTOL UAV?

A conventional fixed-wing aircraft is efficient during forward flight but requires a runway, launch mechanism, or other method of achieving sufficient airspeed.

A multirotor can take off and land vertically and can hover, but its propulsion system generally remains active throughout flight and therefore has different endurance and cruise-efficiency characteristics.

A hybrid VTOL aircraft attempts to combine the advantages of both architectures.

The aircraft considered in this project uses a **dual-system VTOL configuration**:

* dedicated vertical-lift propulsion for take-off, landing and hovering;
* a separate forward-flight propulsion system for fixed-wing flight.

This architecture is selected as the initial project configuration because it allows the vertical-lift and forward-flight propulsion systems to be studied separately.

The configuration also provides a useful engineering platform for investigating the interaction between:

* mechanical design,
* aircraft aerodynamics,
* propulsion,
* flight control,
* electronics,
* autonomous perception,
* and collision avoidance.

The selected configuration is a preliminary project decision and can be reconsidered if later analysis demonstrates that another configuration is more appropriate.

# 0.4 Project Objective

The overall objective is to develop and document a preliminary-to-validation workflow for a hybrid VTOL UAV capable of:

1. Vertical take-off.
2. Vertical climb.
3. Transition to forward flight.
4. Efficient fixed-wing cruise.
5. Autonomous or assisted flight.
6. Detection of potential obstacles.
7. Estimation of obstacle position or relative motion.
8. Prediction of potential collision.
9. Generation of an appropriate avoidance response.
10. Continuation or recovery of the mission.
11. Transition back to VTOL operation.
12. Vertical landing.

The project will initially focus on engineering analysis and simulation.

Physical implementation and experimental validation will be added progressively as the design matures.

# 0.8 Initial Mass Assumption

An initial MTOW of 5 kg is used to initiate the preliminary aircraft-sizing calculations.

This value is an engineering assumption rather than a measured aircraft mass.

The initial mass budget is:

| Subsystem  | Initial Mass | Classification         |
| ---------- | -----------: | ---------------------- |
| Structure  |      1.50 kg | Preliminary assumption |
| Battery    |      1.40 kg | Preliminary assumption |
| Propulsion |      0.85 kg | Preliminary assumption |
| Avionics   |      0.30 kg | Preliminary assumption |
| Payload    |      0.90 kg | Preliminary assumption |
| **Total**  |  **4.95 kg** | Preliminary estimate   |

The difference between the 5 kg initial MTOW assumption and the 4.95 kg subsystem estimate is currently treated as preliminary design margin.

This mass budget will be replaced by component-level estimates as the design progresses.

The final MTOW will be recalculated after:

* mechanical design,
* propulsion selection,
* battery selection,
* avionics selection,
* structural sizing,
* sensor selection,
* and CAD mass-property analysis.

If the calculated aircraft mass changes significantly, the aircraft will be resized and the relevant calculations repeated.

# 0.10 Why Dimensions Are Not Defined First

Aircraft dimensions cannot be selected independently.

For example:

* aircraft mass affects required lift;
* required lift affects wing area;
* wing area and aspect ratio affect wing span;
* wing chord affects Reynolds number;
* Reynolds number affects airfoil performance;
* propulsion requirements affect motor and propeller dimensions;
* propeller dimensions affect motor placement and structural geometry;
* battery energy affects battery mass;
* battery mass affects aircraft mass and centre of gravity;
* component placement affects CG;
* CG affects stability;
* structural requirements affect spar and fuselage dimensions.

Therefore, the first CAD model cannot be considered the final design.

The project uses a sequence of progressively refined design models.

```text
Initial requirements
        ↓
Preliminary calculations
        ↓
First-order geometry
        ↓
Concept CAD
        ↓
Mass properties / CG
        ↓
Aerodynamic analysis
        ↓
Propulsion analysis
        ↓
Structural analysis
        ↓
Detailed CAD
        ↓
Integrated simulation
        ↓
Prototype
        ↓
Validation
```

This is an iterative process.

# 0.11 Preliminary Design vs Detailed Design

The project distinguishes between two major design stages.

## Preliminary Design

The purpose of preliminary design is to determine whether the proposed aircraft concept is physically and technically feasible.

Typical outputs include:

* approximate MTOW;
* wing area;
* wing span;
* chord;
* aspect ratio;
* airfoil candidate;
* approximate tail dimensions;
* approximate propulsion requirements;
* approximate battery requirement;
* approximate fuselage envelope;
* preliminary CG;
* preliminary structural loads.

## Detailed Design

The purpose of detailed design is to convert the preliminary aircraft into a manufacturable and analysable system.

Typical outputs include:

* detailed SolidWorks geometry;
* spar dimensions;
* ribs;
* skins;
* motor mounts;
* fasteners;
* electronics mounts;
* battery enclosure;
* payload bay;
* structural materials;
* manufacturing details;
* final component locations;
* detailed mass properties.

The transition from preliminary to detailed design will occur only after the major design variables have been sufficiently constrained.

* cost;
